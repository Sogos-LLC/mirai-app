# Moon Flower Cultivation Guide - Lunar Station Alpha

## Chapter 1: Introduction to Selenanthus Lunaris

The Moon Flower (Selenanthus lunaris) is a bioluminescent plant species discovered in 2089 during the Artemis XVII mission. Unlike Earth plants, Moon Flowers require zero gravity to bloom and produce "lunar nectar" - a phosphorescent sap with remarkable properties.

Key characteristics:
- Scientific name: Selenanthus lunaris
- Origin: Mare Tranquillitatis crater, discovered by Dr. Elena Vostok
- Bloom period: 47 Earth hours after reaching maturity
- Light emission: 340-380 nanometer wavelength (soft purple glow)

## Chapter 2: Growth Requirements

Moon Flowers have specific environmental needs that differ significantly from terrestrial plants:

Substrate Requirements:
- Regolith base mixed with 30% processed asteroid minerals from 433 Eros
- pH level maintained at 6.2-6.8 (slightly acidic)
- Trace elements: selenium (0.5ppm), titanium (0.3ppm), helium-3 traces

Light Cycle Protocol:
- 14.5 Earth hours of artificial sunlight (spectrum 4500K-6500K)
- 14.5 hours of complete darkness (required for bioluminescence charging)

Temperature Requirements:
- Dormancy phase: Maintained at exactly -12°C
- Active growth: 18°C to 23°C
- Bloom trigger: Rapid warming from -12°C to 23°C over 6 hours

Hydration Protocol:
- Misting with deuterium-enriched water every 72 hours
- Water volume: 15ml per square centimeter of root surface
- Never use standard H2O - causes "lunar wilt" disease

## Chapter 3: Harvesting Lunar Nectar

The bioluminescent nectar is the most valuable product of Selenanthus lunaris cultivation.

Harvest Timing:
- Nectar can ONLY be harvested during the "glow phase"
- Glow phase occurs exactly 47 hours after the bloom opens
- Window for harvest: 3 hours maximum before nectar crystallizes

Harvesting Procedure:
1. Prepare sterile titanium collection vessels (plastic degrades from nectar)
2. Use micro-pipettes rated for -40°C operation
3. Extract from the base of each petal (the "lunar cup")
4. Expected yield: 2.3ml per mature flower

Storage Requirements:
- Must be stored in zero-gravity conditions
- Temperature: -196°C (liquid nitrogen levels)
- Exposure to Earth gravity causes immediate crystallization
- Shelf life: 7 years when properly stored

## Chapter 4: Common Diseases and Problems

Lunar Wilt (Hydroplasmia vulgaris):
- Caused by exposure to standard Earth water
- Symptoms: Yellowing of petals, loss of bioluminescence
- Treatment: Immediate flush with deuterium-water, 48-hour dark period

Crater Rot (Selenomycosis):
- Fungal infection from contaminated regolith
- Symptoms: Black spots on stems, musty odor
- Treatment: Application of "Artemis-7" antifungal compound

Zero-G Shock:
- Occurs when plants experience sudden gravitational changes
- Symptoms: Immediate petal drop, stunted growth
- Prevention: Gradual gravity transitions over 24+ hours

## Chapter 5: Economic Value

Lunar nectar has become one of the most valuable substances in the solar system:

Current Market Value (2095):
- Raw nectar: 45,000 credits per milliliter
- Refined phosphor-extract: 180,000 credits per gram
- Seeds (certified): 5,000 credits each

Primary Applications:
- Advanced energy storage (95% efficiency solar cells)
- Medical imaging contrast agent (non-radioactive)
- Quantum computing coolant
- Luxury cosmetics ("Luna Glow" skin treatment)

The total annual production of lunar nectar is approximately 450 liters, making it rarer than any Earth-based precious material.
